HY-MATE — AI WORKFORCE OPERATING SYSTEM

Product Vision

Transform Hy-Mate from an AI Employee Marketplace into a complete AI Workforce Operating System for businesses.

The core idea is:

Businesses should not have to manage individual AI tools or manually coordinate AI agents. They should define business goals, and Hy-Mate's AI Manager should plan, delegate, execute, review, and report work through a coordinated AI workforce.

The product must feel like a serious premium SaaS platform—not an AI chatbot collection.

The system should combine:

Company Brain
AI Manager
AI Department Managers
AI Specialists
Agent-to-Agent Orchestration
Goal Management
Task Management
Workflow Automation
Human Approval Center
AI Quality Control
Business Integrations
Autopilot
Workforce Analytics
ROI Tracking
AI Employee Marketplace
AI Workflow Marketplace

1. BRAND POSITIONING

Product Name: Hy-Mate

Primary Positioning: Your AI Workforce, Working Together.

Alternative supporting message: One AI Manager. A complete digital workforce.

Product Category: AI Workforce Operating System

Do NOT position the product primarily as:
- AI chatbot
- Prompt marketplace
- AI tools directory
- Collection of AI personas
- Generic AI assistant

The product should communicate:
Goal → Strategy → Delegation → Execution → Approval → Results → Optimization

2. DESIGN DIRECTION

Create a premium, modern, enterprise-ready SaaS interface.

Design characteristics: Minimal, Clean, Premium, Modern, High trust, Spacious, Professional, Strong visual hierarchy, Excellent typography, Subtle animations, Elegant cards, Rounded but not overly playful, Excellent responsive behavior, Desktop-first SaaS dashboard, Fully responsive on tablet and mobile.

Avoid: Cartoonish AI robots, Excessive gradients, Excessive glassmorphism, Generic ChatGPT clone appearance, Overly colorful dashboards, Cheap-looking startup UI, Excessive animations, Fake statistics, Fake testimonials.

The interface should feel closer to a premium combination of Stripe, Linear, Notion, Ramp, modern enterprise AI software than a typical AI wrapper.

8. GOAL SYSTEM

Move the product from task-driven to goal-driven.

Users should be able to create a Business Goal.

Examples: Increase organic traffic by 30%, Generate 100 qualified leads, Increase ecommerce revenue by 20%, Reduce customer support workload, Launch a new product, Increase conversion rate

Each goal contains: Goal name, Description, Target, Deadline, KPI, Current value, Target value, Progress, Assigned AI Manager, Supporting departments, Supporting agents, Tasks, Workflows, Results

Visualize: Goal ↓ Strategy ↓ Projects ↓ Tasks ↓ Agents ↓ Execution ↓ Results

9. COMPANY BRAIN

Create a major product feature called Company Brain.

This is the shared business intelligence layer.

Sections:

Business Profile: Company name, Website, Industry, Business model, Products, Services, Locations, Pricing, Target markets

Brand Knowledge: Brand voice, Messaging, Positioning, Style guidelines, Logo, Brand colors

Audience: Customer personas, Target audience, Pain points, Customer journeys

Competitors: Competitor websites, Competitor information, Market positioning

Business Goals: Revenue targets, Lead targets, Marketing targets, Growth targets

Knowledge Base: Allow users to upload PDFs, Documents, SOPs, Product catalogs, Brand guides, FAQs

Connected Data: Display connected services: Google Analytics, Google Search Console, Shopify, WordPress, Google Ads, Meta Ads, HubSpot

Every AI employee should access only the information necessary for its role.

10. AI WORKFORCE

Header: Your AI Workforce

16. APPROVAL CENTER

Create a dedicated approval inbox.

Header: Approval Center

Example cards:

Ads Agent - Create campaign: Budget: $500 - Buttons: Approve, Edit, Reject
CRM Agent - Send follow-up campaign to 240 leads. - Buttons: Approve, Edit, Reject

17. AI QUALITY CONTROL

Create a QA layer.

Every important AI output should be reviewed by an AI Quality Agent before execution.

QA checks: Accuracy, Brand consistency, Business rules, Formatting, SEO requirements, Factual claims, Duplicate content, Compliance, User-defined policies

Output: QA PASSED or QA FAILED with explanation and required changes.

Failed outputs should automatically return to the responsible agent.

18. AUTOPILOT

Create an autonomy control.

Three levels:
Manual - AI recommends only.
Assisted - AI performs work after approval.
Autopilot - AI executes approved categories automatically.

Allow users to configure autonomy per workflow.

Example: SEO research: Autopilot. Blog publishing: Approval required. Google Ads budget changes: Approval required. Financial transactions: Manual approval.

AI Workflows - Complete automated processes.

The marketplace should include: Search, Categories, Filters (Popular, New, Recommended, Business type, Industry, Price, Complexity)

Each marketplace item should display: What it does, Capabilities, Required integrations, Estimated execution time, Required approvals, Pricing, Rating, Number of installations

Do not use fake ratings or fake installation numbers.

26. NOTIFICATIONS

Create a notification center.

Notifications: Approval required, Goal milestone reached, Workflow failed, Agent needs information, Integration disconnected, New opportunity found, Task completed, QA failed, Important business alert

Allow: In-app, Email, Slack, Future notification channels

27. SECURITY

Build the UI architecture for: Workspace isolation, Role-based permissions, Agent permissions, Integration permissions, Audit logs, API key management, Data access controls, Human approval rules

Create: Settings → Security with: Users, Roles, Permissions, Connected accounts, Agent permissions, Audit logs, Security settings

28. MULTI-TENANT ARCHITECTURE

The application must be designed as a multi-tenant SaaS.

Each workspace must have isolated: Company Brain, Users, Agents, Tasks, Goals, Workflows, Integrations, Analytics, Billing, Files, Activity

Never expose data between workspaces.

29. BILLING

Create pricing architecture around workforce capacity rather than unlimited tasks.

Suggested structure:
Starter - AI Manager, Up to 2 AI employees, Basic workflows, Limited integrations
Growth - AI Manager, Up to 10 AI employees, Advanced workflows, Integrations, Approval Center, Analytics
Business - Full AI workforce, Advanced automation, Custom workflows, Advanced analytics, Priority execution
Enterprise - Custom agents, SSO, Advanced security, Dedicated infrastructure, Custom integrations, Enterprise controls

Use a usage-based architecture internally so AI infrastructure costs can be controlled. Do not hard-code fake billing logic.

30. DATABASE ARCHITECTURE

Design the application around these core entities: users, workspaces, companies, company_brain, departments, agents, agent_capabilities, goals, projects, tasks, task_dependencies, workflows, workflow_nodes, workflow_runs, approvals, integrations, integration_permissions, knowledge_documents, agent_memory, agent_activity, qa_reviews, notifications, analytics, roi_metrics, marketplace_items, subscriptions, usage_records, audit_logs

Use proper relationships and workspace-level ownership.

31. IMPORTANT AI ARCHITECTURE

Do not make every agent a separate generic chatbot.

Create a reusable agent architecture. Agent has: Role, Instructions, Capabilities, Tools, Permissions, Memory, Knowledge access, Department, Manager, Workflows, Approval requirements

Agents should use tools rather than simply generating text.

32. TOOL-CALLING ARCHITECTURE

Build the application so agents can eventually call: APIs, Databases, Search, Website crawlers, Analytics, CMS, CRM, Advertising platforms, Communication tools, Automation platforms

Do not pretend these tools work if backend connections have not been implemented. Build clean interfaces/placeholders for future integrations.

33. AGENT PERMISSION SYSTEM

Every agent must have explicit permissions.

Example: SEO Agent:
READ: GSC, GA4, Website
WRITE: SEO project, Content briefs
CANNOT: Spend advertising budget, Send emails, Publish without approval

This is critical for safety and trust.

34. HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP SYSTEM

The user must remain in control.

High-risk actions require approval. Examples: Publishing, Sending bulk email, Spending advertising budget, Deleting data, Changing important business settings, Financial actions

The AI should explain: What it wants to do, Why, Expected result, Potential risk

Then: Approve / Edit / Reject

35. PRODUCT LANGUAGE

Use professional business terminology.

Prefer: "Goal", "Strategy", "Workforce", "Agent", "Execution", "Approval", "Workflow", "Business Context", "Company Brain", "Business Outcome"

Avoid excessive: "magic", "AI superpower", "revolutionary", "next-level", "crazy", "robot employee"

The product should feel trustworthy.

36. RESPONSIVE DESIGN

The entire application must work on: Desktop, Laptop, Tablet, Mobile

On mobile: Convert sidebar into navigation drawer, Preserve important metrics, Make approval actions easy, Make AI Manager easily accessible, Optimize workflow visualization for touch

37. PERFORMANCE

Prioritize: Fast page loads, Lazy loading, Efficient database queries, Pagination, Optimistic UI where appropriate, Proper loading states, Error states, Empty states

Never show broken or unfinished UI.

38. EMPTY STATES

Create useful empty states.

39. ERROR HANDLING

45. MOST IMPORTANT PRODUCT PRINCIPLE

Do not build Hy-Mate as: "100 AI employees that answer prompts."

Build Hy-Mate as: "An AI operating system that runs business work through coordinated AI employees."

The user should increasingly feel: "I tell Hy-Mate what I want to achieve, and Hy-Mate figures out how to get it done."

That is the ultimate product experience.

46. IMPLEMENTATION PRIORITY

Build in this order:

P0 — Core MVP: Company Brain, AI Manager, Goals, Workforce, Agents, Tasks, Agent activity, Basic orchestration, Approval Center, Demo data

P1 — Execution: Tool calling architecture, Integrations, Workflow Builder, QA Agent, Agent permissions, Autopilot

P2 — Intelligence: Agent-to-agent collaboration, Morning AI Meeting, ROI, Workforce analytics, Business opportunities

P3 — Marketplace: AI Employee Marketplace, AI Workflow Marketplace, AI Templates, Custom Agents, Third-party marketplace

47. CRITICAL LOVABLE INSTRUCTION

Do not try to build fake backend functionality simply to make the UI appear complete.

If a feature requires an external API, database function, authentication provider, payment system, AI API, or integration that is not currently connected:
Build the correct UI architecture.
Create the appropriate data model/interface.
Clearly mark the functionality as pending integration.
Never claim that the integration is actually working.
Keep the application usable in demo mode.
Prioritize clean architecture over fake functionality.
